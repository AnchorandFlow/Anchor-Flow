## ACCESSIBILITY

### 1–2. What's costing the 71, derived from code

Two systemic patterns dominate; everything else is secondary.

- **F-72 — High.** `App.jsx:647,659,671` (`THEMES.calm/coastal/night`) + 331 call sites (`grep -c "T.textFaint"` → 161, `"T.textSoft"` → 170). `T.textFaint` and `T.textSoft` are the app's muted-text tokens, used for empty states ("No tasks yet"), captions, badges, timestamps, and secondary labels across every tab in all three themes. Computed WCAG contrast (relative-luminance formula, verified by script, not eyeballed):

  | Theme | textFaint vs surface | textSoft vs surface |
  |---|---|---|
  | Calm | **2.06:1** | **3.26:1** |
  | Coastal | **2.33:1** | 4.8:1 (passes) |
  | Night | **1.72:1** | **3.24:1** |

  All fail the 4.5:1 AA-normal-text threshold; `textFaint` fails even the 3:1 AA-large-text threshold in every theme (against `bg` it's slightly worse: 1.89–2.12:1). This is the single largest, most pervasive contrast defect in the app — it isn't one component, it's a design-token problem that recurs on nearly every screen. **Why:** the tokens were tuned for aesthetic softness, not measured against a contrast checker. **User impact:** low-vision users can't reliably read empty-state copy, timestamps, or secondary labels on any tab, in any theme. **Fix:** darken `textFaint`/`textSoft` per theme until they clear 4.5:1 against both `bg` and `surface` (e.g., Calm `textFaint` needs roughly `#6f6f80` to clear 4.5:1 against `#FDFAF5`). **Effort:** 2h (recompute + spot-check all three themes). **Expected improvement:** likely the single biggest lever on the A11y score, since axe/Lighthouse's contrast check fires per element and this token pair appears hundreds of times.

- **F-73 — High.** No file:line list needed beyond the counts: `aria-label` appears **6 times** total across `App.jsx`+`components/`+`shell/`; `aria-labelledby` and `role="button"`: **0**; out of 683 `<button>` and 848 `onClick=` occurrences, ~98 render only an `<Icon>` or a bare glyph (✕ × ⋮ 🧭) with no accessible name. Representative: `src/shell/CompassFab.jsx:59` (the app-wide "Ask Compass" FAB is a plain `<div onClick>` with no `role`, no label, no keyboard handler at all — not even reachable by Tab); `App.jsx:4295` (modal close button, icon-only); `App.jsx:4461-4463` (three adjacent icon buttons per task row); `App.jsx:5883/5893` (calendar prev/next chevrons); the shared `SecHead` back-button (`App.jsx:4258-4262`, reused at 8+ call sites). **Exception, and good news:** the actual tab bar (`App.jsx:756-769` data, rendered at `11706-11754`) pairs every emoji with a visible text label — nav is not an offender. **Fix:** add `aria-label` to every icon-only interactive element; make `CompassFab`'s root element a real `<button>`. **Effort:** 3-4h across the codebase (mechanical but high-volume). **Expected improvement:** large — "Buttons have an accessible name" is one of the highest-weighted individual audits in Lighthouse's accessibility score.

- **F-74 — High.** `htmlFor`: **0 hits anywhere in the repo**. `id=` on `<input>`: 5 hits total. Every visible `<label>` (77 in App.jsx, 34 in AnchorVault.jsx) is a sibling, e.g. `App.jsx:1053`: `<div><label>Emoji</label><input .../></div>` — visually adjacent, zero programmatic association. Additionally, several controls have **no label at all**, not even unassociated: `App.jsx:981/996` (`type="color"` swatch pickers, `title=` tooltip only), `App.jsx:1152-1160` (TidePool add-chore inputs + `<select>`, placeholder-only), `App.jsx:1265` (person color swatch, opacity-0 overlay, no label), `App.jsx:7414` and `App.jsx:9971`/`11373` (recipe-picker, event-type, and calendar-person `<select>`s, no label text nearby). **Fix:** wrap every label/input pair (`<label>Text<input/></label>`) or add matching `id`/`htmlFor`; add `aria-label` to the label-less controls. **Effort:** 4-6h (highest-volume item in this audit). **Expected improvement:** large — "Form elements have associated labels" is another top-weighted Lighthouse audit, and this codebase currently fails it at essentially every input.

### 2. Contrast — computed ratios (script-verified, not impressions)

| Pair | Ratio | AA normal (4.5:1) | AA large (3:1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gold `#B08C3D` on Cream `#FDFBF5` (Celebration modal, `App.jsx:9683`) | **3.05:1** | Fail | Pass |
| Gold `#B08C3D` on Navy `#182B45` | 4.53:1 | Pass (barely) | Pass |
| White on Navy `#182B45` (actual button, `App.jsx:9685`) | 14.28:1 | Pass | Pass |
| `T.textFaint`/`T.textSoft` vs surface, all 3 themes | 1.72–4.8:1 | Fail (5 of 6) | Fail (2 of 6) |

Important nuance: the exact "gold on navy" pair named at kickoff **does not occur as text-on-background anywhere in the current code** — the only place `#B08C3D` and `#182B45` co-occur (`Celebration()`, `App.jsx:9647-9689`) uses navy as an opaque *button background* with *white* text (14.28:1, fine); gold text only ever sits on cream there. Other, differently-valued "gold-on-navy" pairs used elsewhere (`TK.gold #c8a97a` on `TK.navy #0e1b2e` in `CompassFab.jsx`, `G.gold #C8A96E` on `G.navy #1B3A5C` in `SafeHarbor.jsx`) computed at 7.75:1 and 5.18:1 — both comfortably pass. So: **F-72 (textFaint/textSoft) is the real, systemic contrast problem; the Celebration modal's gold-on-cream is a real but isolated fail** (folded in as **F-78 — Low**, cross-ref F-72, since it's one component vs. hundreds of instances). Cormorant Garamond at small italic sizes (the empty-state copy pattern) is exactly where `textFaint` gets used, so this compounds rather than being a separate issue.

### 3–4. Keyboard, focus, and ARIA

- **F-75 — Medium.** `App.jsx:4284-4300` (`ModalBox`, the shared modal used by dozens of features) and `AnchorVault.jsx:6579,6780` (its own independent `modalBg` implementation): neither has `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal="true"`, `aria-labelledby`, an Escape-key handler, or a focus trap/initial-focus-on-open. `ModalBox` does correctly lock `document.body.style.overflow` on mount/unmount (`4285-4289`) — background scroll is handled — but Tab can still reach content behind the backdrop-blur, and there's no programmatic way for a screen-reader user to know a dialog opened. Counter-example showing the pattern is fixable: `SunsetClose.jsx:80` has `aria-label="Close"` on its close button — the fix pattern already exists in the codebase, just not applied to `ModalBox`/`AnchorVault`. **Fix:** add `role="dialog"` + `aria-modal="true"` + `aria-labelledby={titleId}` to `ModalBox`; add an Escape handler that calls `onClose`; move focus to the modal (or its first focusable child) on mount and restore it to the trigger element on close. **Effort:** 2h for `ModalBox` (fixes the majority of modals at once since it's shared), +1-2h for `AnchorVault`'s separate implementation.
- **F-76 — Medium.** `aria-live`: **0 hits in the entire src/ tree.** `role="status"`/`role="alert"`: **0 hits.** The sync-status indicator (`App.jsx:11316-11320`, driven by `syncStatus` state) and the in-app toast banner (`showInAppBanner`, `App.jsx:3829-3833`, rendered `11598-11607`) both update visible text/color with zero announcement to assistive tech — a screen-reader user gets no signal that sync failed or that a notification just appeared. The banner is at least `position:fixed` (`11599`) so it doesn't cause layout shift, just silence. **Fix:** wrap the sync-status text in `role="status" aria-live="polite"`; same for the banner container. **Effort:** 30m.
- **F-77 — Medium.** `App.jsx:756-769`/`4255-4266`: landmark roles (`<nav>`, `<main>`, `<header>`, `role="navigation"`) — **0 hits** in `App.jsx` or `index.html`; the entire live app renders inside a bare `<div id="root">`. Heading hierarchy: **no `<h1>` exists anywhere in the live React tree** (the only `<h1>` in the codebase is a string built for an exported/printed packing-list document, `AnchorVault.jsx:2748` — not part of the rendered app); the shared `SecHead` component renders section titles as `<h2>` (used for Calendar, Weekly Rhythm, Shopping, Career, etc.), modals use `<h3>`. So the live app has an h2/h3-only hierarchy with no top-level heading and no landmarks for a screen-reader user to jump between regions. **Fix:** add one `<h1>` (visually-hidden is fine) for the app/current-section name; wrap the bottom nav in `<nav aria-label="Main">`, the tab content area in `<main>`. **Effort:** 1h.

### 5. `prefers-reduced-motion`

- **F-79 — Low.** **0 hits** for `prefers-reduced-motion` anywhere in CSS or JS/JSX. Animation surface confirmed: the Celebration confetti (`App.jsx:9647-9689`, 70 animated pieces + a scale-in pop, both unconditional), `CompassFab.jsx:53/59` (`afBreathe`, a 3s infinite "breathing" animation on the always-visible FAB — never stops, runs for the entire session), `CompassFab.jsx:133-135` (three infinitely-pulsing typing-indicator dots), plus slide-down toasts/banners and a fade-up tab-entry class (`App.jsx:11536-11538`). **Fix:** wrap all of the above in a `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` override that disables/shortens them; simplest single fix is a global CSS rule that neutralizes `animation`/`transition` duration when the media query matches, then re-enable specific safe transitions if needed. **Effort:** 1h.

## UX

### 8. Layout shift sources beyond the School tab

- **F-82 — Medium.** `App.jsx:5623-5649` (AnchorTab "Top 3 — focus here first" card). The card computes `picks` synchronously from real data (calendar, tasks, meals, notifications) first; **only if `picks.length < 3`** (line 5633) does it fall back to `aiSuggestions.todos` — which is `null` until the async `/api/claude` call (`loadAiSuggestions`, the F-18 finding from Session 3) resolves, hundreds of ms to a few seconds later. On any day with fewer than 3 real items (common — a light day with no events), the card **initially renders short (or shows the "Nothing urgent" empty state) and then grows** once the AI response lands, with no reserved height/skeleton. This is a genuine, code-confirmed CLS mechanism that the reported measurement (School tab, CLS 0) never touched, since it's Anchor-tab-specific and depends on data sparsity. **Fix:** reserve a min-height for the card while `aiLoading` is true, or skip the layout change by rendering a skeleton row instead of nothing. **Effort:** 1h. The in-app banner (`App.jsx:11599`) is confirmed `position:fixed` — **not** a CLS source, ruled out directly.

### 9. First-run experience

- **F-83 — Medium.** `App.jsx:3360`: `showOnboardingWizard` defaults to `false`; comment at `3555` states plainly "wizard only from Settings" — a brand-new user is **never shown the onboarding wizard automatically**; it only exists for someone who digs into Settings and triggers it manually. Compounding this: `hasExistingData` (`3554`) and `SAMPLE_TASKS` (`3560`) are both declared but **never referenced anywhere else in the file** — dead code with no first-run detection path at all wired up. So a brand-new user with zero data lands directly on the Anchor tab's empty-everything state (reasonable empty-state copy exists per-section — "No tasks yet — add one or tap ✨ Plan", "No events today — open space 🌿" — these are not broken, just never explained as a *first* experience) with no tour, no sample data, no "here's what to do first." Compounding further: **Cluster B** (already logged, Session 2 — hardcoded developer family data in `PERSON_COLORS`, calendar filters, `CalEventFormModal` defaults) means several features silently misbehave for the very first household that isn't the developer's own, right when a new user is most likely to notice something's off. **Where they'd get lost, concretely:** Settings → Family setup is the only path to a working app, but nothing on first load points there; School tab (`App.jsx:9792`, confirmed via direct read) does correctly redirect with "Add children to your People list in Settings" — that pattern exists and works, it's just not applied as the *entry* experience. **Fix:** wire `hasExistingData` to actually gate something (either show `OnboardingWizard` automatically when `!hasExistingData`, or show a lighter first-run banner pointing to Settings); remove the dead `SAMPLE_TASKS` if truly unused, or wire it as an actual demo-data option. **Effort:** 3-4h.

### 10. Consistency

Spot-checked several less-central tabs (SchoolTab, CareerSection) against the main flow: **empty states are actually well-executed and consistent** — italic Cormorant Garamond + a calm emoji + a specific next action is the pattern nearly everywhere (`"No licenses yet — add your professional licenses and certifications."`, `"No events today — open space 🌿"`, SchoolTab's empty state even includes a working "Go to Settings" CTA button). This is a genuine strength, not a gap — see the Strengths section below; a consistency review that only lists problems would misrepresent the app. The one concrete inconsistency found: the update/sync-apply mechanism is a full `window.location.reload()` (10+ call sites, e.g. `App.jsx:2091,2221,2238,2571,2617,2655,2718`) rather than an in-place UI update — **F-84 — Low.** This is meaningfully mitigated by `afUserIsEditing()` (`App.jsx:2479-2486`, correctly checks focused-input-type, recent-typing, and open-modal state before allowing a reload), so it won't yank a field out from under active typing — but when it *does* fire, it's a jarring full navigation rather than a graceful in-place refresh, and there's no warning toast beforehand. **Fix:** consider a "New data available — tap to refresh" banner pattern (the `showInAppBanner` infrastructure already exists) instead of a silent auto-reload, at least for the sync-pull case. **Effort:** 2h.

### 15. Analytics

## Strengths (for Session 5's "done exceptionally well" section)

Two things stood out clearly enough, and were confirmed thoroughly enough, to flag as genuine strengths rather than absence-of-problems:

- **Empty states are consistently well-crafted across the entire app.** Spot-checked across AnchorTab, the Weekly Rhythm glance card, SchoolTab, and CareerSection — the same voice recurs everywhere without apparent central enforcement: italic Cormorant Garamond, a calm/nature emoji, and a specific next action, e.g. `"No events today — open space 🌿"`, `"🌙 Nothing planned yet — keep it easy tonight"`, `"No tasks yet — add one or tap ✨ Plan"`, `"No licenses yet — add your professional licenses and certifications."` SchoolTab's empty state goes further and includes a working "Go to Settings" CTA button (`App.jsx:9790-9800`) rather than just an explanation. This level of consistency, across features built at different times by what looks like a single author working alone, is unusual and worth preserving deliberately (e.g., as a documented pattern/component) rather than leaving it as an accident of a consistent voice.
- **Landing-page pricing copy is clear and pre-empts the most likely point of confusion.** `index.html:379-408` — a clean `$0` forever-free tier against `$12.99/mo · or $119/yr` for "Anchor & Flow Plus," full feature lists per tier, and critically an explicit clarifying line: `"One Plus subscription covers your entire household — not per person. This is good, deliberate copywriting, not a gap to fix.
